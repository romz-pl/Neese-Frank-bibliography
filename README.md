# Professor Frank Neese

## Overview

Frank Neese is a German theoretical chemist and the lead author of ORCA, one of the most widely used quantum chemistry programs. He was born on 13 December 1967 in Wiesbaden, Germany.

### Career

Neese trained in Germany and then worked abroad. He earned his PhD at the University of Konstanz and was a postdoctoral fellow with Edward I. Solomon at Stanford University, where he began writing ORCA. He returned to Konstanz for his habilitation, then took a staff position at the Max Planck Institute for Bioinorganic Chemistry in Mülheim an der Ruhr. He moved to the University of Bonn in 2006 as chair of theoretical chemistry, returned to Mülheim in 2011 as a director of what was then the Max Planck Institute for Chemical Energy Conversion, and in 2018 moved to the Max Planck Institute for Coal Research (formerly the Kohlenforschung institute), where he heads the Department of Molecular Theory and Spectroscopy. The sources I checked describe that institute under both names, which refer to the same Mülheim institute. He also holds an honorary professorship in computational chemistry at the University of Duisburg-Essen.

### ORCA

Neese began writing ORCA in 1997–1999 while at Stanford, and development has continued through each of his subsequent positions. The name came from a whale-watching trip he took on the California coast, after he decided against yet another "whatever-Mol-something" name. ORCA is a general-purpose package covering semi-empirical methods, density functional theory, many-body perturbation theory, coupled cluster, and multireference methods. Its input format is designed to be easy to learn, which makes advanced methods accessible to researchers who are not specialists in quantum chemistry.

ORCA is free for academic use. In 2016 Neese co-founded FACCTs GmbH, a spin-off of the Max Planck Society, to license the software commercially to industry and to offer consulting and support. The program is developed mainly by his group at the institute and by FACCTs, with contributions from an external community of developers. Neese has said it is among the most widely used quantum chemistry programs worldwide, with tens of thousands of registered users.

### Research focus

His research covers method development in ab initio quantum chemistry, including local correlation, open-shell systems, and multireference methods. He applies these methods to transition metal chemistry, metalloproteins, catalysis, energy conversion, and molecular spectroscopy, especially core-level spectroscopy and magnetic properties. He has published several hundred papers, and his work is often cited in coordination chemistry and bioinorganic chemistry.

### Recognition

He received the Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize in 2010, the most prestigious research award in Germany. He is a member of the Leopoldina (the German National Academy of Sciences), the International Academy of Quantum Molecular Science, and the Academia Europaea. He is married to the spectroscopist Serena DeBeer, who is also a director at a Max Planck institute.
